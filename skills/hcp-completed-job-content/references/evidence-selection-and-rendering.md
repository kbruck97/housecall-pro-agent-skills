# Evidence selection, photo review and visual QA

## Selection rubric

| Class | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| hero | Finished work in a clear usable composition | Lead image if job/context and rights match |
| proof | Measurement, test setup, labeling or workmanship detail | Explain the observed step within its evidentiary limits |
| documentation | Nameplate, model sticker, internal record | Usually supporting evidence only; redact serial/identifiers |
| unusable | Unclear, blurry, dark, unsafe disclosure or unsuitable crop | Skip; no invented reconstruction presented as proof |

Interesting notes may support a text card: a documented space constraint, verified technology change, a supported failure explanation, or work hidden after completion. Ordinary “job completed” notes plus a box photo rarely support a useful story. Do not invent a richer story to meet cadence.

## Claims from photos

A gauge image can show a reading at a moment, not automatically instrument calibration, hold duration, leak tightness, code compliance or long-term performance. Corroborate instrument/units, measured system, timing, technician notes and relevant approved process before explaining a procedure. “A documented evacuation check during commissioning” may be supportable; “this proves a perfect installation and competitors skip this” is not.

A neatly labeled panel supports a limited workmanship observation, not a whole-installation safety guarantee. A model/nameplate rating is a product specification, not verified household savings. Any efficiency/savings/health/safety claim requires appropriate supporting sources and the tenant's review gate. Customer praise is not permission to publish a testimonial.

Create a claim ledger with: exact proposed wording; source observation; source locator/hash; confidence/limitations; source/claim expiry; reviewer; status (`supported`, `qualified`, `blocked`). Remove or qualify unsupported clauses, not merely add a vague disclaimer elsewhere.

## Privacy and rights inspection

Inspect full image and crops for customer names, faces, voices, minors, house numbers, plates, distinct interiors, paperwork, invoice/financial data, addresses/contact details, equipment serials, lockbox/access codes, GPS/EXIF metadata and private reflections/screens. Examine combustion printouts and handwritten notes, not just filenames.

Use tenant permission policy for both the job story and media. Releases should identify the grantor's authority, subjects/assets, allowed purposes/channels, limitations, term and revocation status. A technician's upload is not homeowner consent; a customer's consent does not automatically cover an employee's face. Even where consent permits identifiable use, the generic draft default remains de-identified. Retain raw source only in authorized private storage with a retention rule; distribute sanitized derivatives.

Cropping/redaction must not alter the claimed evidence. If blurring a gauge destroys the proof, use narrower wording or reject the candidate. Strip metadata from public derivatives; preserve necessary provenance privately. Do not recreate “cleaned-up” documentary photos with invented equipment, missing components or synthetic before/after states.

## Authenticity and media count

Use actual same-job, same-system, correctly ordered images for before/after; document both capture references and why they match. Two similar units or photos from different addresses are not a pair. Label both panels visibly. If only one image exists, use one image. Do not imply video footage exists when only still documentation was supplied. Illustrations, if separately approved, must be clearly illustrative and must never masquerade as actual job evidence.

Historical source yields reflected one limited harvest, not universal rates. Measure current sample denominator, jobs with any media, usable-photo count, proof/hero distribution and unknown rights. Do not infer absent service photos means the business performs no service work. Recommend a short technician capture checklist—problem, finding, completed work, one takeaway, consent-aware media—without creating a field workflow or asking customers automatically.

## Rendering guidance

Use the tenant brand pack, not inherited agency colors or fonts. A square 1200×1200 layout can be a local draft option, not a claim about current platform requirements.

Photo-led layout: subject-biased crop, restrained type, bottom gradient scrim with adequate contrast where text actually sits, short headline and small kicker. Text card: simple background, clear hierarchy, one supported takeaway, approved business/service area. Do not add a fake job photo.

Apply EXIF transpose before crop. Avoid occluding the equipment/proof subject. For before/after, draw labels last or reserve protected regions so brand plates cannot hide either label. Inspect actual rendered images, not only successful renderer exit status. Check text bounds, wrapping, collision, contrast, crop, spelling, safe zones and channel-specific constraints. Add accurate accessible alt text without disclosing redacted identities.

Track master media digest, derivative digest, transform recipe/version and visual reviewer. A new crop or wording change alters the approved package; it needs fresh validation and, if already approved, reapproval. Never claim visual review if only text or filenames were inspected.
